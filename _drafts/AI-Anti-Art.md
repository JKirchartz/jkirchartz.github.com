AI Art is Anti-Art
==================

Wikipedia states that "Anti-art is a loosely used term applied to an array of
concepts and attitudes that reject prior definitions of art and question art in
general.  Somewhat paradoxically, anti-art tends to conduct this questioning
and rejection from the vantage point of art." -- as an art school student,
philosophy nerd, and life long fan of the DADAists (the mother of all Anti-Art) I
believe this "Anti-Art" term can be accurately applied to AI Art.

Literature is Art -- it's one of the main parts that early AI was trained on; I
personally participated in National Novel Generation Month between 2015 and
2021, and am considering participating now in 2026; and applied algorithms and
algorithmic training to a variety of publicly domain texts. But, I admit some
are dubious on copyright, like [The Beatles
Bible](https://jkirchartz.com/NaNoGenMo/2017/) -- I'd say my use was
transformative fair-use quotations of the lyrics, not sure a lawyer or a court
would agree; But I digress, back to my point: If Literature is Art, then the
prompt written into the AI is an Art. But the output of the AI is still
Anti-Art.

There's a Balinese saying like "We have no art, we do everything as beautifully
as possible," according to Sam Keen -- in their traditional culture there is no
division between creating art and everyday life. This quote was initially
brought to my attention by Mierle Laderman Ukeles "Care: Maintenance Art" with
"we try to do everything well" where she engages with Maintenance as a type of
Performance Art. But this quote was also mentioned in Marshall McLuhan's
"Understanding Media." with "we do everything as well as possible;" briefly
afterwards McLuhan states "a technological extension of our bodies designed to
alleviate physical stress can bring on psychic stress that may be much worse."

Surely AI Output is aesthetically pleasing to its the person who wrote the
specification, like any customized product that hasn't been fully realized
until it's in the customer's hands.

I suppose, in a way, there's a sort of western view that could make "AI Art"
into Art; AI strives towards a type of perfection -- but it's a stunted
perfection: user happiness is the intent and the final form of "AI Art" is
curated by them, the image is a secondary response to the initial Literary Art
provided by the user's prompt, the prompt may be edited several times over
(another part of the literary process); but the final image, while curated, is
still Anti-Art.

Somebody working to produce images through AI and then process them further in some
other way -- that would be an artistic endeavour -- but the image output from
the AI is still Anti-Art; and the artist may well be performing another form of Anti-Art.
